What Is ABA?

The science of helping children grow

Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) is an evidence-based therapy that helps children build communication, social, and life skills, while reducing challenging behaviors that get in the way of learning and connection.

ABA looks at each child as an individual. It finds the what and the why behind behaviors, then replaces the ones holding your child back with skills that move them forward.

We serve children and young adults ages 2 to 21.

Early Intervention (Ages 2 to 6)

Building foundational communication, play skills, and school readiness from the ground up.

Pre-Adolescent (Ages 7 to 12)

Expanding communication, building life skills, and supporting independence at home and in school.

Adolescent and Young Adult (Ages 13 to 21)

Focusing on independence, self-care, social skills, and preparing for adult life.

How It Works

What to expect

We want families to feel informed and prepared. Here is an honest look at the process from first contact to first session.

Step 01

Reach Out

Fill out our contact form or have your child's physician or school submit a referral. We complete an intake and verify your insurance benefits.

Step 02

Authorization and Assessment

We work with your insurance to obtain authorization for an initial assessment. Once approved, your BCBA conducts a thorough evaluation of your child.

Step 03

Treatment Plan

Your BCBA develops an individualized treatment plan based on the assessment. That plan is submitted to insurance for approval before services begin.

Step 04

Services Begin

Once your treatment plan is approved, therapy starts. Your family is involved every step of the way, with regular caregiver training built into the process.

A note on timelines: Insurance authorization can take time and is largely outside our control. We will keep you informed at every stage and advocate on your behalf throughout the process.

FAQ

Questions we hear most

If you do not see your question here, reach out. We are always happy to talk.

What is ABA therapy? +
Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) is an evidence-based therapy that helps children build communication, social, and life skills while reducing challenging behaviors. It is the most researched and widely recommended treatment for children with autism, endorsed by the US Surgeon General and the American Academy of Pediatrics.
How do I get started? +
Fill out our contact form and we will reach out within one to two business days. You can also have your child's physician or school professional submit a referral. From there, we will walk you through every step of the process.
Does my child need an autism diagnosis? +
Yes. A formal diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is required by insurance providers to authorize ABA services. If your child does not yet have a diagnosis, we can help point you in the right direction to get one.
Do you accept Medicaid? +
We are currently completing our Medicaid enrollment and are also credentialing with select commercial insurance providers. Reach out to us and we can discuss your specific insurance situation and timeline.
What ages do you serve? +
We serve children and young adults from ages 2 to 21. Our approach is tailored to each stage of development, from early intervention through adolescence and into young adulthood.
Where do sessions take place? +
All sessions are delivered in your home. We believe children learn best in the environment where they actually live. Community-based sessions, such as practicing skills at a park or store, may also be incorporated into your child's plan.
What is expected of me as a caregiver? +
Family involvement is a core part of what we do. Caregivers participate in regular training sessions so they can support their child's progress between sessions. You do not need any prior experience with ABA. We teach you everything you need to know.
Is ABA therapy a cure for autism? +
No. ABA is not a cure, and we would never present it as one. What ABA does is help your child build skills and reduce barriers so they can participate more fully in daily life. Progress looks different for every child, and we celebrate every step forward, no matter how small.
